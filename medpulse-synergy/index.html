QUESTION / CLINICAL VIGNETTE
A 54-year-old male with a history of long-standing hypertension presents with sudden tearing chest pain radiating to the back. CXR shows widened mediastinum. What is the most critical initial diagnostic step & first-line blood pressure control agent?
Click card or press Spacebar to reveal answer
HIGH YIELD ANSWER & DIAGNOSIS
Diagnosis: Aortic Dissection (Type A or B)

Initial Diagnostic Test: CT Angiography of the Chest (or TEE if renal failure).

First-line Medical Treatment: IV Beta-blocker (e.g., Esmolol or Labetalol) to lower heart rate and aortic wall shear stress (target HR < 60 bpm) before vasodilators.
Mayo Clinic Board Tip: Always lower Heart Rate BEFORE SBP using Labetalol/Esmolol to prevent reflex tachycardia!
